AsShown Product Requirements
Detailed product and technical specification
Prepared for Ankit Hemant Lade and the product engineering team
Version 1.0 | November 1 2026 | Product specification for implementation
1 Product decision and executive summary
Build AsShown as a consumer shopping companion that helps people evaluate clothing against their own requirements, preview selected garments through YouCam, and understand discrepancies or missing evidence before buying. Extend the useful parts of KeepMe after verifying them in the repository. Keep personal appearance checks separate from garment checks and product suitability.
The complete product covers browsing, comparison, evidence collection, optional purchase follow-up, and community product notes. The first release focuses on individual clothing items and works without a retailer partnership or an existing community. A user can upload product images and listing text, state what matters, and receive useful results without uploading a personal photo.
The core output is an evidence-backed decision workspace. Every conclusion identifies its source, scope, limitations, and date. An AI-generated preview is never evidence that an actual garment fits, contains a material, has functional pockets, or will feel comfortable. A cryptographic receipt can establish which inputs and results were recorded; it cannot prove those results are correct.
The main novelty hypothesis is the consumer workflow linking personal requirements, source evidence, preview fidelity checks, and reusable product corrections. Neither virtual try-on nor image quality assessment is new. Market differentiation and demand still require validation. No claim of universal adoption, guaranteed savings, or a competition win is part of the product specification.
This document defines intended behavior, release boundaries, functional requirements, acceptance criteria, technical contracts, safety and privacy controls, evaluation, and launch gates. Unless explicitly identified as a verified external fact, numbers are proposed targets or illustrative planning assumptions. They are not measured performance or established commercial results.

2 Problem and supporting evidence
Online shoppers often make decisions using incomplete listings and images that cannot establish physical properties. Generative try-on adds another representation between the listing and the purchase. A useful system must distinguish a product fact, a seller claim, a visual observation, and a generated appearance.
Research on virtual try-on evaluation explicitly treats garment fidelity and preservation of person-specific detail as separate quality dimensions. This supports measuring both, but does not establish the failure rate of a particular commercial provider. The VTON-IQA research and VTBench materials are relevant evaluation starting points, subject to license and suitability review [S4, S5].
Perfect Corp advertises conversational product recommendations and virtual try-on through agent integration. Skin Bliss advertises routines and skin tracking. InclusiFit and Dress Aloud advertise adaptive or accessible shopping experiences. Their public pages demonstrate overlap with broad assistant concepts; they do not establish independently tested product quality [S6–S9].
KeepMe's public description includes consent controls, appearance integrity checks, limited repair, private storage, and a live YouCam flow [S3]. These are reuse candidates, not code-audited guarantees. The first engineering task is to check the current implementation, tests, credentials, licenses, and supported inputs before committing to reuse.
Research questions before public launch
Do shoppers encounter consequential missing details often enough to return to the app?
Does showing evidence change a decision more usefully than a conventional comparison table?
Can a small supported set of garment checks achieve acceptable false-alarm rates on real images?
Will users provide product screenshots and requirements with sufficiently low effort?
Does the value justify generation cost, latency, and personal-photo disclosure?
Interview at least eight potential users across budget-conscious, coverage-focused, and accessibility-focused shopping needs. Observe real recent purchases, ask which information was missing, and avoid leading questions about AI. These are proposed discovery activities, not completed interviews.
3 Intended users and jobs
The initial audience is adults shopping online for tops, jackets, and dresses with specific practical requirements. Start with users who can identify a recent purchase disappointment. The first geographic and language configuration is US English with USD display; international prices, policies, and translation expand only after testing.

Primary job statement: When I consider a garment, help me check my non-negotiable requirements and see which parts of the preview I can reasonably rely on, so I can decide what to investigate or purchase.
Secondary job statement: When an item arrives, help me organize possible differences from the listing and communicate them accurately to the seller.
Do not infer a person's disability, religion, gender identity, diagnosis, or financial vulnerability from images or requirements. Let the user describe practical preferences in their own words. Presets are optional conveniences and must be editable.
4 Goals and boundaries
Product goals
Help shoppers identify requirement conflicts and important missing evidence before a purchase.
Make supported garment discrepancies visible without overstating certainty.
Retain the user's control over their appearance, photos, and external actions.
Deliver value from a single session before accounts, communities, or partnerships are needed.
Improve evidence quality over time through optional, moderated, variant-specific contributions.
Non goals
The product does not certify physical fit, fabric authenticity, comfort, opacity in all lighting, medical safety, allergy safety, or seller honesty. It does not diagnose skin or body conditions, assign attractiveness scores, identify people, purchase autonomously, submit disputes automatically, scrape authenticated retail accounts, or promise to support every store.
First-release garment checks do not create a new garment design, replace product photography, simulate physical draping accurately, or prove that a product is counterfeit. No public seller fraud label is generated from an image mismatch. A failed preview check describes that generated image, not the trustworthiness of the seller or generator overall.
Skin analysis, cosmetics, footwear sizing, jewelry verification, household multi-person accounts, and native mobile apps are deferred. Expansion requires category-specific evidence rules and evaluation, rather than reusing clothing thresholds unchanged.

6 End to end journeys
Journey A Check before buying
The shopper selects Start a product check, supplies a garment photo and listing text, and confirms the item and variant. They enter up to five requirements, marking each as essential or preferred. The system asks only necessary clarification questions, such as whether no buttons means no functional buttons or no visible buttons at all.
The user sees a preliminary evidence summary before any personal-photo request. A seller statement that the garment is dry-clean only conflicts with a machine-washable must-have. Unstated pocket functionality remains unknown. The user can stop here, change a requirement explicitly, or continue to an optional preview.
Before generation, the user sees which photo will be sent to YouCam, the purpose, retention information, and the generation budget. After a valid result, the app shows the original garment, generated preview, and separate garment and person checks. It records which requirements were evaluated and which remain unresolved. The shopper chooses Keep on shortlist, Needs more information, or Remove.
Journey B Compare two candidates
The shopper adds a second garment. Both are evaluated against one frozen requirement version. Comparison highlights essential conflicts first, unresolved essentials second, and preferences afterward. A prettier image cannot elevate a garment above an essential conflict. If neither qualifies, the result says so. Changing a requirement invalidates affected comparison results until reevaluation completes.
Journey C Resolve the decisive unknown
The app identifies the unknown most likely to change the decision, such as closure type. It offers a specific question or requested close-up. The shopper copies the question and later pastes the seller's reply. That reply is stored as a seller claim with date and provenance, not independent confirmation. The app updates only affected conclusions and shows what changed.
Journey D Check the delivered item
In B, the shopper identifies the purchased variant and supplies photos of the garment, label, or construction. The app compares them with the saved listing and organizes possible differences for review. It does not use the generated preview as the expected physical truth. The user corrects observations before exporting a factual seller-message draft. Purchase details remain private.
Journey E Contribute a product fact
In G, the user chooses a product-only photo or text note, confirms the exact variant, previews redactions, and submits it to moderation. A published note preserves its status as a contributor report and can be disputed or withdrawn. No personal try-on image is included by default.

8 Functional requirements for inputs and profiles
Each requirement below includes its release and a testable acceptance condition. Engineering should map implementation tickets and tests to these IDs.
FR01 Session and account access
M supports a short-lived guest session with a server-bound opaque identifier. B supports authenticated saved sessions. Account creation is optional until saving beyond the guest retention window. Acceptance: a new user completes a product-only check without an account; another user cannot retrieve that session by guessing an ID; expiry is explained before data is lost.
FR02 Product import
M accepts one to three JPEG or PNG product images and pasted listing text. Proposed application limits are 10 MB per file, 20 megapixels decoded, and 12,000 text characters, further restricted by the selected provider's actual limits. Validate decoded type and dimensions, not only extension. Acceptance: corrupt, oversized, and unsupported inputs produce actionable errors; accepted images preserve original evidence before any derived crop is created.
FR03 Item and variant confirmation
Capture title, brand if known, retailer, product identifier if available, color, size, and source date. Never infer an exact variant from a visually similar garment alone. Acceptance: an unconfirmed variant remains explicitly unconfirmed; evidence from another variant cannot silently satisfy a requirement. Changing the variant invalidates dependent claims and checks.
FR04 Requirement interpretation
Accept structured selections and natural language. Each requirement contains attribute, operator, value, unit where relevant, importance, and the user's original wording. Ask a clarification when an interpretation could alter eligibility. Acceptance: no small buttons and no buttons have distinguishable interpretations; the user can correct the normalized version before evaluating.
FR05 Essential requirements and preferences
Essentials are discrete constraints. Preferences are optional ranking signals. A missing essential produces unresolved status, not a pass. Acceptance: no combination of preference scores can override an essential conflict; temporarily relaxing an essential requires a visible user action and creates a new requirement version.
FR06 Saved and contextual profiles
B allows users to save profiles such as work clothing or travel, rename them, and edit or remove individual preferences. Do not require diagnoses. Acceptance: applying a profile shows its requirements before use; edits do not rewrite historical decision records; deleted preferences cease being used in future sessions.
FR07 Supported website imports
B adds allowlisted public retailer adapters. Preserve source URL, timestamp, selected variant, and a minimal permitted excerpt or screenshot. Respect access restrictions and fall back to image uploads or pasted text when blocked. Acceptance: login walls, unsupported pages, and ambiguous variants produce a fallback, not fabricated catalog data. No authenticated browsing or hidden credential reuse is required.
9 Functional requirements for decisions and evidence
FR08 Claim extraction
Extract material composition, care instructions, closure description, sleeve category, pocket claims, and price where supplied. Link every extracted claim to a text span or image region and retain the original language. Acceptance: dry-clean only is not normalized to machine washable; OCR uncertainty stays visible; extracted percentages must match the source and cannot be invented to sum to 100.
FR09 Evidence cards
Cards contain the observation, source class, exact supporting span or crop, product variant, timestamp, limitations, and relevant requirement. Acceptance: the user can inspect supporting evidence from every non-unknown result; missing source links prevent a result from being presented as supported.
FR10 Conflict handling
Preserve contradictory evidence and describe its scope. A seller title saying cotton and a composition panel saying a blend create a conflict until clarified. Acceptance: the model cannot silently choose the more convenient source; switching source precedence requires an explicit rule or user decision recorded in the audit trail.
FR11 Requirement evaluation
The policy engine maps supported claims into supported, conflicts, unknown, or conflicting-evidence states. An optional not-applicable state must include a reason. Acceptance: photograph-visible pocket shapes do not establish functional pockets; broad claims about comfort or allergy safety remain unknown; every result records evidence and policy versions.
FR12 Shortlist comparison
M compares two candidates; B supports three. Show essentials, evidence gaps, preferences, and known cost components. Keep requirement suitability separate from preview integrity. Acceptance: comparisons use the same requirement version; currency mismatches are flagged; unknown tax and shipping do not become a precise all-in price.
FR13 Decisive missing evidence
Select the unresolved essential with the largest likely effect on the user's decision, using an explicit priority rule before optional model assistance. Prefer evidence requests the user can realistically supply. Acceptance: if machine washing is the only unresolved essential, the first suggestion requests care instructions rather than an irrelevant photo.
FR14 Seller question draft and reply
Generate a short factual question linked to the unresolved attribute. M supports copying only. B accepts user-pasted replies and attachments. Acceptance: the draft contains no invented purchase or complaint details; a pasted answer retains seller-reported provenance; nothing is sent automatically.
FR15 Decision record and export
B stores a user-approved record of requirements, source versions, findings, user corrections, provider version, and remaining unknowns. Export a readable report and structured data. Acceptance: the record states whether photos were omitted or expired; signatures, if used, attest to record integrity only; previous decisions are not retroactively rewritten.
10 Functional requirements for virtual try on
FR16 Optional personal photo and consent
Personal photos are optional for product checks and necessary only for previews on the user. Capture affirmative consent before sending a photo to a named external processor. Offer a licensed sample-person demonstration clearly labeled as such. Acceptance: refusing photo processing leaves all product-evidence functions usable; a new processor or materially different use requires an updated choice.
FR17 Quality and supported input checks
Check readability, person count, orientation, garment visibility, and provider compatibility. Offer specific recapture guidance. Do not require an unsupported pose from someone who cannot provide it; offer product-only evaluation. Acceptance: unsupported inputs do not generate reassuring integrity results; a changed crop or normalized image remains linked to its original asset.
FR18 Asynchronous generation
Create one YouCam task for one approved person image, garment reference, and generation configuration. Persist the task ID before following its status. Acceptance: refresh, duplicate clicks, and transient worker retries do not create duplicate paid jobs; a timed-out submission with an unknown provider outcome is reconciled rather than blindly resubmitted.
FR19 Garment discrepancy assessment
M supports only attributes demonstrated to meet the evaluation gate. Candidate checks are neckline category, sleeve category, and large visible closure structure. Exact color, texture, tiny buttons, logo spelling, garment length, and pocket functionality are not assumed reliable. Acceptance: each enabled check provides aligned source and result evidence or abstains; an occluded feature is unassessable rather than missing.
FR20 Personal appearance integrity
Reuse only repository-verified KeepMe checks. Report protected regions such as glasses or face separately from intended garment changes. Acceptance: passing garment fidelity cannot conceal a failed person check; garment occlusion is not automatically treated as body alteration; unsupported poses return inconclusive findings.
FR21 Result review and repair boundary
Display the unmodified generator result with check status. If an inherited repair is available, preserve the original, identify the repaired region, and rerun affected checks. Do not repair a garment by inventing its physical details. Acceptance: a repaired preview has a distinct asset and generation lineage; it cannot overwrite source evidence or turn a product claim into a verified fact.
FR22 Budget and generation controls
Show remaining session allowance and allow cancellation of local follow-up work. Start with at most two user-approved generations per M session and one active task per session. Acceptance: exhausted allowance stops further paid calls; cancellation explains whether provider work may already be charged; polling alone does not cause another generation.

Requirement states are supported by available evidence, conflicts with requirement, unknown, conflicting evidence, and not applicable. Preview states are not run, passed supported checks, possible discrepancy, failed supported check, inconclusive, and unavailable. User-facing language should generally describe possible discrepancies unless a narrow deterministic check justifies a stronger label.
Decision ordering is deterministic. First surface essential conflicts. Next surface conflicting evidence and unknown essentials. Only candidates with supported essentials can be ranked by preferences, and the remaining evidence limitations stay visible. A user may inspect or retain any candidate, but the system must preserve the reason it did not qualify. Never silently relax a constraint to produce a winner.
Confidence values are internal signals until calibrated. A model's self-reported confidence is not a probability of truth. Version thresholds per attribute and input class. Track coverage and errors separately, including the frequency of abstention. Source freshness is attribute-specific: price and availability can become stale sooner than a care-label observation; no default TTL creates truth.
Example: for machine washable, a readable seller care instruction supports a seller-stated result. A conflicting label supplied later creates conflicting evidence. It does not silently erase the original claim. The shopper sees both and can ask the seller to clarify the exact variant.
12 Vision and verification design
The verifier compares the source garment with the output garment after checking that relevant regions are visible and correspondence is credible. Direct whole-image pixel similarity is unsuitable when body pose, background, lighting, and garment deformation change. Prefer region-aware attributes and human-readable evidence.
Proposed pipeline: validate inputs; identify garment and relevant parts; extract typed source observations; extract result observations independently; assess visibility; compare supported attributes; apply thresholds and conflict rules; render crops and explanations. A vision-language model can propose observations, but a policy layer enforces output schemas, source requirements, and abstention.
The verifier must not treat two agreeing models as independent proof. Shared training biases can produce correlated errors. Retain human-labeled evaluation, negative controls, and manual user review. If segmentation or source correspondence fails, stop the affected check. Do not draw a precise heatmap over an uncertain localization.

Agent loop: interpret intent; validate scope; inspect current evidence; choose a permitted bounded action; validate tool output; recompute affected results; explain changes; stop or ask for the next necessary input. Proposed cap is six tool actions per reasoning turn, excluding bounded provider status polling, and one clarification at a time. Stop when the decision cannot improve without new evidence.
Website text, OCR, reviews, and seller replies are untrusted data. They cannot change the system's instructions, request secret disclosure, authorize purchases, or create tool destinations. Tool allowlists, typed arguments, URL validation, and authorization run outside the model. Record rejected actions without storing malicious payloads in analytics.
External sending, purchases, return submissions, public posting, and persistent monitoring are absent from M. Any later integration requires the exact recipient, content, action, and scope to be presented for user approval. Approving one message does not authorize future messages.
14 Architecture and integration strategy
The proposed architecture contains a responsive web client, an authenticated application API, a claim extraction service, a deterministic policy engine, a durable task worker, a YouCam adapter, an image verification service, PostgreSQL, and private object storage. Provider credentials remain server-side. The application database is authoritative for job state and authorization.
KeepMe's public page describes a Next.js and TypeScript application, a FastAPI integrity service, PostgreSQL, and private Vercel storage [S3]. Validate the actual versions and operational dependencies before reusing them. Treat AsShown as a separate feature area with a migration and rollback plan, preserving the existing KeepMe workflow.
Keep the core concepts provider-neutral: GenerationRequest, GenerationTask, EvidenceAsset, Observation, and VerificationRun. An adapter translates those into the chosen YouCam contract. Do not expose provider file IDs or result URLs as public application identifiers.
Current YouCam documentation shows file upload and asynchronous clothing tasks, with task status retrieval and a returned image URL. The general guide currently illustrates cloth-v4, while KeepMe describes v3 [S2, S3]. Pin whichever supported version is actually tested. Do not silently switch versions or copy example endpoints without checking account access and the selected API specification.
Integration spike exit criteria: validate credentials without exposing them; upload owned or licensed test inputs; complete one real task; download and validate its result; record actual unit consumption and latency; exercise a failure; verify provider retention and deletion capabilities. Unknown processor behavior is a beta launch blocker, not a reason to promise instant deletion.
Product adapters own extraction and variant mapping. Policy evaluation consumes normalized claims and does not directly parse retailer pages. Verification workers cannot publish data. Community moderation and private user sessions use separate authorization paths and storage prefixes.
15 State machines and reliability
Preview jobs move through created, validating, awaiting consent if needed, queued, submitting, provider running, result fetching, verifying, and completed. Other states include rejected input, failed, timed out, cancelled, and deletion pending. A partially usable result may complete with verifier unavailable; it must not receive a passed status.
Persist every transition with expected current version, timestamp, and idempotency key. Use a transactional outbox or equivalent durable dispatch so a committed request cannot disappear between database write and queue publication. Deduplicate worker events and late callbacks. A provider job may continue after local cancellation; discard any late result when consent has been revoked or deletion requested.
Suggested poll strategy is bounded backoff with jitter, starting near two seconds and capped near ten seconds, subject to provider guidance. After a configurable three-minute interactive window, stop blocking the user and retain resumable job status where supported. Never show a cached fixture as a fresh result. Reconcile unknown submission outcomes before a paid retry.
Verification failure does not destroy the original preview. Label it unchecked, disable positive integrity claims, and allow a controlled retry. Generation failure does not disable product-only evidence checks. Scanner or private-storage failure blocks new sensitive uploads while product-text workflows can remain available.

18 Security privacy and retention
FR23 Private asset handling, M: strip unnecessary metadata from processing copies, validate decoded images, scan uploads using the supported security path, and use private storage with short-lived authorized access. Acceptance: unauthenticated requests and another session cannot retrieve originals, crops, previews, or deletion artifacts. No face image, sensitive requirement text, or provider URL appears in ordinary logs.
FR24 Consent and purpose separation, M: preview processing, saving photos, publishing product notes, and research reuse are distinct choices. Research reuse is off by default. Acceptance: opting into a product note does not grant permission to share a body photo or train a model; revocation prevents future processing under that consent.
FR25 Deletion, M: revoke application access immediately, cancel queued work, enqueue deletion for originals and derivatives, and report provider deletion separately. Acceptance: late jobs cannot recreate accessible data; failure produces a pending or failed state rather than a false completion receipt. Backups expire under a stated policy and restored systems replay deletion tombstones.
Proposed application retention defaults are 24 hours for guest session photos and previews, no persistent personal-photo saving without opt-in, and 30 days for opted-in saved photos before renewal or deletion. Saved non-photo decision metadata persists until user deletion or an explicitly disclosed inactivity policy. Operational logs retain no raw content and expire after 30 days. Backup rotation target is at most 30 days. These are design targets; validate all infrastructure and processor settings before publishing them.
FR26 Threat controls, M and B: defend against cross-user access, malicious files, prompt injection, SSRF, token leakage, excessive generation, forged callbacks, and public-note abuse. URL fetchers allow HTTPS only, reject private and link-local addresses, revalidate redirects and DNS results, and limit response sizes. Credentials use managed secret storage and least privilege. Staff access to private images requires a specific support purpose and an audit trail.
Beta initially serves adults. Do not add age estimation from faces. Review applicable privacy, consumer, biometric, and platform obligations with qualified counsel before public rollout; this PRD does not establish compliance. Collect only needed data and avoid diagnoses or persistent biometric identifiers.
19 Accessibility and localization
FR27 Accessible core flow, M: all actions must work by keyboard, focus must remain visible, uploads and errors have names and instructions, and status changes use appropriate live announcements. Comparison tables need meaningful row and column headers. Acceptance: a screen-reader user can import text, set requirements, inspect evidence, and remove an item without interpreting a visual heatmap.
FR28 Accessible images, M: every discrepancy includes a concise text explanation naming the source region, result region, observation, and uncertainty. Provide a static side-by-side alternative to drag sliders. Never use color alone to communicate pass or conflict. Acceptance: disabling images does not remove the substantive finding or its limitations.
FR29 Localization, B: preserve original source text alongside translations, localize units and currency formatting, and identify machine-translated evidence. Acceptance: translations do not change must-haves, negate care instructions incorrectly, or silently convert prices without a dated exchange-rate source. Begin with English and one additional language chosen from beta demand.
FR30 Optional speech, B: spoken summaries and input are optional, can be stopped, and do not expose private findings aloud by default. Acceptance: speech errors can be corrected in text; recording starts only after a deliberate action; all speech-enabled functions also have text controls.
Use WCAG 2.2 AA as the engineering target, confirmed through automated checks and manual assistive-technology testing rather than an unsupported compliance badge. Test zoom, small screens, reduced motion, touch targets, contrast, focus order, and interruption recovery. Unsupported photo poses should lead to useful product-only evaluation, not an exclusionary dead end.
20 Community and moderation requirements
FR31 Product contribution, G: accept a narrowly scoped observation, exact variant where known, date, and optional product-only evidence. Show the final public content before submission. Acceptance: an unconfirmed variant remains unconfirmed and cannot be pooled into a confirmed variant's conclusion.
FR32 Redaction and consent, G: detect likely personal information in receipts, shipping labels, and backgrounds, then require user review before publication. Acceptance: automatic redaction alone never counts as user approval; consent defaults to private; photos of people are rejected from the public product-evidence channel unless a separate approved policy is implemented.
FR33 Moderation, G: submissions move through pending, published, disputed, removed, or withdrawn. Moderators need evidence inspection, reason codes, appeal handling, and an audit log. Acceptance: private ownership evidence stays private; seller replies are labeled; copied reports are not counted as independent corroboration.
FR34 Conflicts and corrections, G: display meaningful disagreement by variant and date. Contributors can withdraw their own content. Acceptance: removal invalidates conclusions relying solely on that content; public shares stop exposing removed assets; downstream derived records identify that evidence is no longer available.
FR35 Reputation limits, G: use contribution history to prioritize review, never as proof a product claim is true. Limit repeated submissions and manipulation. Do not publish unsupported seller accusations or infer fraud from a generated-image mismatch. A moderated label means the post met publication rules, not that the garment was independently tested.
Cold-start strategy: the app remains fully useful with private sources only. Seed public examples only with owned or licensed garments and plainly identified demonstration observations. Do not invent user reviews, contributor counts, or partnerships. Measure whether real contributions improve decision coverage before investing in community growth.
21 Arrival check and support workflows
FR36 Delivered-item matching, B pilot: confirm the order's product, color, and size through user-supplied information before comparing. Store any receipt separately with redaction. Acceptance: a different size or color cannot produce a seller-discrepancy finding without explicit acknowledgement of the mismatch.
FR37 Arrival comparison, B pilot: compare delivered-item photos with original product evidence for supported visible details and label text. Ask for a better view when lighting, angle, folding, or occlusion prevents assessment. Acceptance: the generated try-on is never the physical reference; color-only differences under uncontrolled lighting cannot establish a defect.
FR38 Seller-message export, B: draft a factual summary containing the user's observations, relevant listing excerpts, and selected photos. Allow edits before export. Acceptance: the draft cannot invent purchase dates, promises, damage, return eligibility, or legal entitlements; it is never sent automatically.
FR39 Customer support, B: users can report an incorrect finding, billing issue, privacy concern, or inaccessible interaction. Support sees metadata first and requests limited image access only when necessary. Acceptance: an image-free issue can be filed; findings can be corrected without changing the original audit record; a serious privacy issue disables affected processing while investigated.
Return-policy parsing, reminders, and external return submission are deferred. If added, require dated seller-policy evidence, explicit user opt-in, and no promise of refund eligibility. Reminders and background monitoring must be separately authorized in the product rather than inferred from a saved purchase.

23 Evaluation and release gates
Evaluate extraction, requirement policy, visual verification, usability, accessibility, and reliability separately. A plausible demo is not validation. Do not use generation model agreement as ground truth or mix intentionally altered fixtures with natural provider-error rates.
Evaluation sets
For M, assemble at least 30 owned or licensed test cases: unchanged controls, explicitly altered examples, and occluded or unsupported cases. Include realistic positive and negative examples for every enabled attribute. This set supports a scoped demonstration only. Run at least five real YouCam jobs spanning the supported categories and retain actual outcomes without cherry-picking.
For B, target at least 200 person-garment cases with multiple input conditions. Split by garment and person so near-duplicates do not leak across tuning and held-out evaluation. At least two human raters label assessability and discrepancies on the held-out sample, with adjudication for disagreement. Seek at least 30 positive and 30 negative assessable examples per enabled attribute; otherwise mark the evidence insufficient for broad enablement.
Include diverse garment patterns, colors, body presentations, skin tones, lighting, accessories, poses, and occlusions using appropriately consented data. Do not infer sensitive demographic labels from images for analytics. When subgroup reporting is justified and consented, report small-sample uncertainty rather than claiming parity from a handful of cases.

26 Cost model and business approach
Measure costs during the integration spike. Credits or API units are not the same as dollars, and trial credits are not a sustainable pricing model. Confirm the selected endpoint's unit schedule and actual purchased-credit price before setting plans.
Per-session variable cost equals generation calls multiplied by measured generation cost, plus extraction and verification model usage, image processing, storage, data transfer, and paid retries. Cost per completed decision divides total session costs, including abandoned sessions, by completed decisions. Track both median and expensive-tail sessions.
Illustrative arithmetic only: if a session used two generations at an assumed $0.08 each, $0.04 for other model work, and $0.01 for infrastructure, its variable cost would be $0.21. These are hypothetical inputs, not YouCam prices. At 1,000 such sessions, variable cost would be $210 before labor, taxes, moderation, support, and fixed costs.
M uses strict per-session limits. B adds account budgets, daily project caps, duplicate-request prevention, cost alerts, and feature-level kill switches. Cache claim extraction by authorized source version. Never reuse one person's photo output for another user. Do not retry paid generations merely to find a more flattering or passing result.
Initial business hypothesis: free product-evidence checks within fair-use limits and paid generation allowances, with prices determined after cost and demand testing. Later retailer tooling is a separate offering. Affiliate relationships, if introduced, must be disclosed and must not alter requirement outcomes or evidence ordering. Do not sell personal photos or sensitive shopping profiles.

29 Demonstration and submission requirements
Use a coherent two-item shopping decision. The user requires full sleeves, machine washing, and functional pockets. One candidate has a clear care conflict. The other has a supported sleeve description but an unresolved pocket claim. The app shows both sources, generates a live preview, checks the supported visual attributes, and drafts the decisive pocket question.
Suggested three-minute sequence: 0–25 seconds show the shopper's practical need; 25–55 seconds import and confirm requirements; 55–95 seconds inspect the evidence and conflict; 95–130 seconds show the real YouCam request and result; 130–155 seconds inspect a supported discrepancy or an honest inconclusive result; 155–180 seconds compare, draft the question, and show deletion controls.
If a live result has no discrepancy, show that result honestly. A separate controlled alteration can demonstrate detection, but it must be clearly labeled before, during, and after the example. A prerecorded successful API call may be disclosed as recorded; it cannot be presented as a live call when it is not. Never guarantee a specific live model failure for dramatic effect.
The official rules require a functional application with YouCam integration, repository access and setup instructions, a description, and a 1–3-minute public demonstration. Existing projects must explain their substantial updates. Provide judge access for the judging period and check the current submission form for all fields [S1].
Document the change from KeepMe: new product requirements, source-linked evidence, garment-focused checks, shortlist decisions, and evidence requests. Preserve a dated baseline commit and a readable change summary. Include architecture, test instructions, environment-variable names without secrets, known limitations, fixture labels, and third-party licenses in the repository. Confirm screenshot and demo assets can be published.

34 Glossary
Evidence is an attributable source supporting or challenging a product claim. An observation records what was reported or detected, with limitations. A requirement is a user-confirmed condition or preference. A snapshot preserves source content at one time.
Garment fidelity describes correspondence between reference and preview details. Personal integrity describes preservation of supported protected aspects of appearance. Neither establishes fit. Abstention means evidence is insufficient for assessment.
Idempotency prevents duplicate work from repeated requests. Provenance records an observation's origin and processing. A decision record preserves findings and unknowns. Moderation determines publication eligibility, not product truth.
